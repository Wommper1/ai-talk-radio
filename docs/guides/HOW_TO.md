## Make a better show

Three things make the biggest difference:

1. **A specific topic.** *"Should restaurants ban phones at the table?"* makes
   a better show than *"phones"*. A question with two sides is ideal.
2. **A different model for each host.** In **Settings**, each host has its own
   **Model** box. Two different models sound like two different people instead
   of one model doing voices.
3. **The right format.** See below.

## Choose the format and length

In **Studio**, under **On air**:

| Format | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Debate** | They disagree and press each other |
| **Interview** | One asks, one answers at length |
| **Roundtable** | Relaxed; they build on each other's points |
| **Banter** | Fast, funny, a little chaotic |
| **Explainer** | One teaches, one keeps them honest |
| **Cold open** | Drops in mid-conversation |
| **Deep dive** | Works through a document you paste in |

**Length** is the number of turns. The screen shows roughly how long that is on
air. Shows are capped at 30 minutes so the final mix stays reliable.

**Music** sets the background mood: Tech, Chill, Newsroom, Late Night, Upbeat,
Noir, Rock, or No music. The music is generated by the station itself, so there
is nothing to download and no copyright to worry about.

## Call in to a live show

1. Start a show.
2. A **Call in** panel appears in the Studio while it is running.
3. Type your name and what you want to say, then send it.

You go on air at the next gap, in your own voice on a phone line, and whoever
speaks next answers you by name. Call in again later under the same name and
the hosts remember you as a regular.

## Make a show about a document

1. In **Studio**, paste text into **Source material**, or press **Load a text
   file**.
2. Set **Format** to **Deep dive**.
3. Type a topic (or a short title for the document) and start the show.

A "producer" step reads the document first and finds the hook, the key ideas,
the surprising moments and good everyday comparisons. The hosts then discuss
it instead of reading it out.

*Tip: plain articles, notes and reports work best. On small models, documents
full of code or AI prompts can confuse it.*

## Change who the hosts are

**Quick:** **Settings → The pair** has three ready-made pairings. Picking one
changes names, personalities and angles, and leaves your voices and models
alone.

| Pairing | Who |
| --- | --- |
| **Drive-time DJs** | Marco works the mic; Dee punctures him |
| **Deep-dive duo** | Alex drives the story; Wren has the realisations |
| **Late-night pair** | Paul is warm and curious; Rae is sceptical |

**Custom:** further down Settings, each host has a **Name**, a **Character**
(who they are) and an **Angle** (the position they argue from). Make the two
hosts different from each other; contrast is what makes it a show.

Press **Save settings** when you are done.

## Give the hosts a shared history

In **Studio → Direction → Who they are to each other**, describe their
relationship, for example:

> Alex and Lacey are married. Twelve years, two kids, one dog. They bicker like
> a couple who know exactly which buttons to press.

Both hosts are told this word for word, and reminded every few turns, so they
never contradict it halfway through the show.

## Steer a show without rewriting the hosts

**Studio → Direction → Producer's note** is added to both hosts' instructions
for this show. Use it for things like *"Keep it family friendly"* or *"Mention
that it's raining all day"*.

## Change the voices

**Settings → Speech** chooses the voice engine. Each host then has a **Voice**
and a **Mic sound** (for example *Studio*, or *Phone-in* for a band-limited
caller sound). Press **Preview** next to a host to hear them.

The station remembers your voice choices separately for each engine, so you can
switch engines without losing them.

## Use the more natural Edge voices

Edge voices sound the most human. They need an internet connection, and each
line is sent to Microsoft to be spoken.

**Windows download:**

1. Install Python 3 from <https://www.python.org>.
2. In the station folder, double-click **Set up Edge voices**. Wait for
   *"Edge voices are ready"*.
3. In the station, open **Settings → Run the setup guide**, press **Check
   again** under voices, choose **Edge voices**, and press **Go to the studio**.

**From source:** run `npm run setup-edge-tts` (Windows) or see the README for
the macOS and Linux command.

With Edge on, **Settings → Voice casting** lets you audition the best voices
for each host side by side and apply the one you like.

## Name your station

**Studio → Direction → Station name.** The hosts use it on air, and the
station's opening jingle and ident are generated from it.

## Turn ad breaks on or off

**Settings → Ad breaks → Take commercial breaks.** You can also set how many
breaks per show, how many spots per break, who reads them, and how much louder
the ads are than the show (just like real radio).

With no setup at all, the station invents funny local businesses to advertise.

## Advertise your own business (or a friend's)

1. **Settings → Sponsors → Add sponsor.** A name is enough; products, talking
   points and a tagline make the adverts better.
2. Fill in **How it should be read**, for example *"breathless hard sell"* or
   *"flat and matter-of-fact, as if none of this were unusual"*. This is the
   single biggest thing that makes each spot different.
3. **Important:** set **Who advertises** to **Mixed** or **My sponsors only**.
   On **Made-up sponsors**, your sponsors are never read.

## Play your own recorded advert

Put a `.wav` or `.mp3` in the station's **`ads`** folder (or use **Add a file**
under **Your own spots**), then point a sponsor at it. That spot plays your
recording instead of a written one, levelled to match the show.

## Open the show with a song

1. Put `.mp3` or `.wav` files in the station's **`music`** folder (or use
   **Add a file**). Name them `Artist - Title.mp3` so the announcer can say
   what they are.
2. Turn on **Studio → Direction → Open with a record**.

The show opens, an announcer throws to the song, and the song plays while the
hosts' first lines are being written in the background. Afterwards the first
host back-announces the track, like a real DJ.

## Make shows faster

**Studio → Direction → Performance profile:**

| Profile | Trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| **Fast Live** | Fewest AI calls; fastest |
| **Balanced** | Keeps memory and notes |
| **Produced** | Full planning; best shows, slowest |

Other things that help:

- Use **Piper** voices instead of Edge (no internet round trip).
- Use a model that fits entirely in your graphics card's memory. One that
  spills into normal RAM can be ten times slower.
- Fewer turns.

## Let the hosts remember earlier shows

**Settings → Memory → Remember previous shows.** The hosts can then refer back
to earlier episodes. **Rebuild** re-reads all your shows, and the search box
shows you what the station remembers.

## Resume a show that was interrupted

If the station or your computer stops mid-show, every finished line is saved.
Next time, **Studio → Interrupted broadcasts** lists it. **Resume** carries on
from the last completed line and reuses the voice clips already made.

## Find, play and download your shows

- **Library** lists every finished show. Open one to play it with its
  transcript.
- **Files** shows every file on disk, with **Download** and **Show the folder
  on disk** buttons.

Each show is a plain folder under `shows/` containing `show.wav` (the finished
mix) and `show.json` (details and the timed transcript).

## Publish your shows as a podcast

The **Podcast** tab turns the shows you choose into a podcast anyone can
subscribe to in Apple Podcasts, Spotify, Pocket Casts or any podcast app. The
station builds a folder; you upload it to a free web host. Your computer never
goes online.

1. **Pick a home for it first.** On <https://github.com/new>, create a public
   repository, for example `my-show`. Your podcast will live at
   `https://YOUR-NAME.github.io/my-show/`.
2. In **Podcast → Your podcast**, type that address into **Web address it will
   live at**. Fill in the title and description, and optionally your own cover
   (a square PNG or JPEG, 1400 to 3000 pixels; otherwise one is drawn for you).
3. Under **Episodes**, tick the shows to publish. Only ticked shows go out.
4. Read **Before you publish**. If an episode plays a song from your music
   folder, it is flagged: only publish it if you own the rights.
5. Press **Publish**. Each show is converted to MP3 (roughly a tenth of its
   length; later publishes reuse it) and the feed and website are written.
6. Press **Open the folder**. In your GitHub repository, click **uploading an
   existing file**, drag in everything in that folder (including `episodes`)
   and press **Commit changes**.
7. In the repository's **Settings → Pages**, choose *Deploy from a branch*,
   `main`, `/ (root)`, and **Save**. A minute or two later your podcast's
   website is at the address from step 1, and its feed at
   `…/feed.xml`.
8. Paste the feed address into a podcast app to subscribe, or submit it to
   [Apple Podcasts Connect](https://podcastsconnect.apple.com) and
   [Spotify for Creators](https://creators.spotify.com) to be listed.

**New episode later?** Tick it, press **Publish** again, and upload the changed
files the same way. Unticking a show removes its files from the folder; upload
again (and delete it from the repository) to take it down.

**Preview the website** shows exactly what listeners will see before you
upload anything.

## Free up disk space

**Files → Delete the per-turn clips, keep the finished show** removes the
individual line recordings, which is most of a show's size, and keeps the
finished mix.

## Back up the station

**Operations → Backup and restore.** A *state* backup saves your settings and
the station's memory. A *full* archive also includes your shows and music.

## Run the setup again

**Settings → Run the setup guide** (top right). Useful after installing new
voices or switching from LM Studio to Ollama.
